Case Study 01 · SaaS Retention

Subscription Churn & Retention Signals

A subscription product wants to know whether onboarding changes made in Q1 actually improved retention, and which cancellation reason to attack first. The question: does the newer cohort hold up better after month 3, and where should the retention team focus?

Finding

The March cohort (new onboarding flow) retains ~14 points higher by month 6 than January — the gap opens right after month 2, when the old flow's drop-off was steepest.

Recommended action

"Missing feature" and "too complex" together drive over half of cancellations — prioritize a guided setup checklist before pricing changes, which only account for 12%.

Case Study 02 · Marketing Analytics

Marketing Channel Efficiency

Finding

Referral has the lowest CAC ($38) and the highest conversion rate (6.1%) by a wide margin — but currently receives the smallest share of budget of the five channels.

Recommended action

Reallocate ~15% of paid-social spend into a referral incentive program; paid social has the highest CAC ($146) for a below-median conversion rate.

Case Study 03 · Operations

Warehouse Delay Root-Cause Analysis

On-time delivery has been slipping for two months. Before proposing a fix, the operations team needs to know which failure point in the fulfillment chain is actually responsible — not the loudest complaint, the most frequent cause.

Finding

Carrier pickup delays alone account for 34% of late shipments — more than the next two causes combined — and the on-time rate began dropping the same week a carrier route changed.

Recommended action

Renegotiate the pickup SLA with the affected carrier or add a second pickup window before investing in warehouse-floor process changes, which address a smaller share of the problem.

Case Study 04 · Public-Data Style Analysis

City Bike-Share Demand Patterns

A city bike-share operator wants to know when and where to rebalance bikes. The question: which hours and stations create the sharpest supply-demand mismatch, so rebalancing crews stop guessing?

Finding

Demand is sharply bimodal on weekdays (AM + PM rush) but flat and midday-centered on weekends — a single rebalancing schedule is serving two very different demand shapes.

Recommended action

Split rebalancing into a weekday rush-hour schedule and a separate weekend midday schedule, prioritizing the top 3 stations that account for a disproportionate share of weekday rush rides.
